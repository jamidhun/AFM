MODULE A Accounting Logic & Journal Entries HIGH YIELD
🧠 The Intuition Behind Double Entry

Every transaction has TWO sides — this is not a rule, it's a law of financial physics. When you buy a machine for ₹1 lakh cash, something came in (machine = asset) and something went out (cash = asset decreased). The equation Assets = Liabilities + Capital must always balance.

Think of it like a see-saw. One side always goes up exactly as much as the other goes down. Your job in the exam is to identify WHAT TYPE of account each side belongs to, then apply the golden rule for that type.

🚨 Biggest Trap — "Bank" in Cash Book

In the Cash Book, when you deposit cash into bank → Bank column is DEBITED (cash came into bank). But in the bank's Pass Book, YOUR account is CREDITED because you are a creditor to the bank. This mirror-image relationship is the #1 source of BRS confusion.

Rule: Cash Book and Pass Book are mirrors. What's Dr in one is Cr in the other. Always ask: "From whose perspective?"

The exam loves asking: "Which concept states: anticipate no profit but anticipate all losses?" Students confuse this with Full Disclosure or Prudence — they are the SAME. Conservatism = Prudence. Don't pick them as two separate answers.

MODULE A·2 BRS — Bank Reconciliation Statement HIGH YIELD
🧠 The Core Intuition

BRS exists because Cash Book and Pass Book are written by different people at different times. Your Cash Book records when YOU act (issue cheque, deposit cheque). The bank's Pass Book records when the BANK acts (pays, receives). There's always a TIME GAP between these two events.

The goal of BRS: Start with one balance and arrive at the other through adjustments.

🗺️ The 4 Types of Differences — Master This
SituationEffect on Cash BookEffect on Pass BookAdjustment (to reconcile)
Cheque issued, NOT yet presented to bankAlready Cr (reduced)NOT yet debitedADD to Pass Book balance
Cheque deposited, NOT yet credited by bankAlready Dr (increased)NOT yet creditedDEDUCT from Pass Book balance
Bank charges levied (not in Cash Book)Not recordedAlready debitedDEDUCT from Cash Book balance
Interest credited by bank (not in Cash Book)Not recordedAlready creditedADD to Cash Book balance
🎯
The Exam Approach: Always start with what's GIVEN and move toward what's ASKED. If given Cash Book balance, adjust it toward Pass Book. If given Pass Book balance, adjust toward Cash Book. The adjustments flip direction.
⚡ The Shortcut Framework

Starting with Cash Book balance → arriving at Pass Book balance:

  • Cheque issued but not presented → DEDUCT
  • Cheque deposited but not credited → ADD
  • Bank charges (not in Cash Book) → DEDUCT
  • Bank interest/income (not in Cash Book) → ADD

Memory: Whatever the bank did that you haven't recorded yet → adjust accordingly. If the bank debited you (charges) and you haven't → your Cash Book is overstated, so deduct.

🚨 Overdraft Trap

When there's a bank OVERDRAFT, all directions flip. An overdraft means you owe the bank — so Credit Balance in Cash Book = Debit Balance in Pass Book. All the addition/subtraction signs in your BRS reverse. Watch for the word "overdraft" in questions — it's a trap specifically designed to catch students who've only memorised the normal case.

A·3 Depreciation — Deep Application HIGH YIELD
🧠 Why Depreciation Exists

Depreciation is not a cash expense — it's a notional charge to spread the cost of an asset over its useful life (Matching Concept + Going Concern). By charging depreciation, you're saying: "This asset helped earn revenue this year, so its cost share goes to this year's P&L."

The asset's book value reduces by this amount each year. This is why depreciation is BOTH an expense in P&L AND a reduction of asset in Balance Sheet.

🗺️ SLM vs WDV — The Critical Comparison

BasisOriginal CostBook Value (reducing)

Asset valueReaches ZERO (or residual)NEVER reaches zero
Recognised byCompanies Act (usually)Income Tax Act

Formula(Cost – Scrap) ÷ LifeBook Value × Rate%
SLM — ANNUAL DEPRECIATION Dep = (Cost – Net Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life (years)

WDV — YEAR N BOOK VALUE BV = Cost × (1 – Rate)ⁿ

SOYD — FRACTION FOR YEAR t Fraction = (Remaining Life) ÷ (n(n+1)/2) e.g., for 5-yr asset, Year 1 = 5/15, Year 2 = 4/15...
📝 Quick Example — SOYD

Asset Cost ₹1,20,000. Life = 4 years. Scrap = ₹20,000. Depreciable = ₹1,00,000.

Sum of digits = 4+3+2+1 = 10.

Year 1 dep = 4/10 × 1,00,000 = ₹40,000 (highest in Year 1).

Year 4 dep = 1/10 × 1,00,000 = ₹10,000 (lowest in Year 4).

Pattern: SOYD accelerates early — same philosophy as WDV but calculated differently.

🚨 Common Trap — "Which method does IT Act recognise?"

Answer is always WDV. The IT Act only recognises WDV (also called Diminishing Balance). SLM is NOT recognised under the IT Act for tax depreciation purposes.

But Companies Act allows both SLM and WDV — companies choose based on their policy.

A·4 Bills of Exchange — Due Date & Entries
🧠 The Logic

A Bill of Exchange is essentially a formal IOU between a creditor (drawer) and debtor (drawee). The creditor says: "Pay me in 3 months." The debtor signs it = Acceptance. Then the creditor holds it, discounts it with a bank, or endorses it to someone else.

DUE DATE CALCULATION Due Date = Date of Bill + Period + 3 Days of Grace e.g., Bill dated 1 March, 1 month → Nominal = 1 April + 3 days = 4 April

IF DUE DATE = PUBLIC HOLIDAY Due date becomes the PREVIOUS WORKING day (not next!)

BILL DISCOUNT AMOUNT Discount = Face Value × Rate/100 × (Remaining Days/365)
🧠 Memory Trick
Drawer = draws (writes) the bill = the CREDITOR (person owed money).
Drawee = one drawn upon = the DEBTOR (person who owes money).
Payee = receives money (usually same as drawer unless endorsed to someone).
Think: Drawer draws a gun — he's in control, he's the creditor.

MODULE B Financial Statements & Bank Accounts HIGH YIELD
🧠 The Flow of Final Accounts

Think of it as a funnel. All transactions → Journal → Ledger → Trial Balance → Adjustments → Final Accounts. Each step is a refinement. The Trial Balance just tests arithmetic (debits = credits). The adjustments bring in all the accrual/matching items. Then you split into Trading A/c, P&L A/c, and Balance Sheet.

THE HOLY GRAIL FORMULA COGS = Opening Stock + Net Purchases – Closing Stock Gross Profit = Net Sales – COGS Net Profit = Gross Profit – Operating Expenses + Other Income

BALANCE SHEET ALWAYS Assets = Capital + Liabilities (or: Capital = Assets – Liabilities)
B·2 Cash Flow Statement — Deep Understanding HIGH YIELD
🧠 WHY 3 Activities?

The 3-activity structure tells you WHERE the cash is coming from and going. A healthy company should ideally have positive operating cash flows (its core business generates cash), negative investing cash flows (it's investing in growth), and managed financing cash flows.

If a company shows positive cash flow ONLY from financing (borrowing) and not operations — that's a warning sign. The examiner knows this — questions may test your ability to classify, not just define.

🚨 The Non-Cash Trap

Bonus share issue is a non-cash transaction — it's just a journal entry moving reserves to share capital. No actual cash flows. Hence it's EXCLUDED from the cash flow statement. Depreciation is also non-cash — but it appears in the Indirect Method as an add-back (since it reduced profit but not cash).

Test yourself: "Does actual cash change hands?" If NO → excluded from cash flow or added back.

🧠 Indirect Method — The Mental Model

Start with Net Profit (which is an accrual figure). Add back non-cash expenses (depreciation, provisions). Adjust for working capital changes (increase in debtors = less cash actually received, so deduct). Arrive at actual operating cash flow.

Net Profit
+ Depreciation (non-cash)
+ Increase in Creditors (more credit = more cash retained)
– Increase in Debtors (more credit sales = less cash received)
– Increase in Stock (bought more = cash went out)
= Operating Cash Flow

🧠 Rebate on Bills Discounted — What Is It?

When a bank discounts a bill (say, 3 months), it earns the full discount upfront but has only earned part of it by year-end. The UNEARNED portion = Rebate on Bills Discounted. It must be shown as a liability because the bank still owes this "service" to maturity.

Think of it like advance receipt — the bank got money for a service it hasn't fully delivered yet.

This is more useful than knowing the raw numbers. In exams, you must know what each ratio MEASURES and what its ideal value means.

Quick Ratio (Acid Test) excludes: Stock/Inventory and Prepaid Expenses. NOT cash. NOT debtors. NOT bills receivable. Many students exclude everything except cash — that's wrong.

MODULE C Financial Management — NPV, IRR, Derivatives HIGH YIELD
🧠 Time Value of Money — The Fundamental Insight

₹100 today is worth MORE than ₹100 a year later. Why? Because ₹100 today can be invested and grow. This means: future cash flows are worth less to us today — we must DISCOUNT them. This single insight drives all of NPV, IRR, bond valuation, and annuity calculations.

Discounting = going backwards in time. Compounding = going forwards. Same formula, different direction.

COMPOUND INTEREST A = P × (1 + r)ⁿ [r = annual rate; n = years]

PRESENT VALUE (Discounting) PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ

RULE OF 72 Years to double = 72 ÷ Interest Rate % e.g., at 8% → 72/8 = 9 years to double

FV OF ORDINARY ANNUITY FV = A × [(1+r)ⁿ – 1] / r [Ordinary = end of period payments]
🚨 Annuity Trap — Ordinary vs Due

Ordinary Annuity = payments at END of each period (most common, more exam questions).
Annuity Due = payments at BEGINNING of each period (slightly more value because each payment earns one extra period of interest).

Annuity Due FV = Ordinary Annuity FV × (1 + r). That's the only adjustment needed.

C·2 NPV & IRR — Decision Tools MOST TESTED
🧠 NPV Intuition

NPV answers: "If I invest in this project today, am I richer or poorer in today's money terms?" Positive NPV = project creates value. Negative NPV = value is destroyed. Zero NPV = break even in present value terms.

The discount rate used is your cost of capital — the return you could have earned elsewhere (opportunity cost). NPV compares what you get vs what you could have gotten.

NPV FORMULA NPV = [CF₁/(1+r)¹ + CF₂/(1+r)² + ... + CFₙ/(1+r)ⁿ] – Initial Investment ``` Rule: Accept if NPV > 0 | Reject if NPV < 0

IRR — THE INTERPOLATION FORMULA IRR = R₁ + [NPV₁ / (NPV₁ – NPV₂)] × (R₂ – R₁) [R₁ = lower rate where NPV is +ve; R₂ = higher rate where NPV is –ve] Rule: Accept if IRR > Cost of Capital ```
📝 IRR Calculation — Step by Step

NPV at 10% = ₹2,00,000 (positive). NPV at 11% = −₹50,000 (negative).

IRR = 10 + [2,00,000 / (2,00,000 – (–50,000))] × (11 – 10) = 10 + [2,00,000 / 2,50,000] × 1 = 10 + 0.8 = 10.8%

Key: NPV₁ – NPV₂ = 2,00,000 – (−50,000) = 2,50,000. Don't forget the double negative!

🚨 IRR Trap — The Double Negative

When NPV₂ is negative, the formula's denominator is NPV₁ MINUS a negative number. Students forget to handle the negative sign and get wrong answers. Always write out the sign explicitly: if NPV at 11% = −50,000, then NPV₁ − NPV₂ = 2,00,000 − (−50,000) = 2,50,000. The range is always positive.

C·3 WACC, CAPM & Capital Structure
🧠 WACC — Why Weighted Average?

A company uses multiple sources of finance — equity (expensive), debt (cheaper, but risky), preference shares. WACC is the blended cost of all these. It's the minimum return a project must earn to satisfy ALL investors. Use it as the discount rate in NPV when the question doesn't specify otherwise.

WACC WACC = (Wₑ × Kₑ) + (Wₚ × Kₚ) + (Wₐ × Kₐ) [W = weight; K = cost; e = equity; p = preference; d = debt]

Example: 40% equity @20%, 10% pref @15%, 50% debt @10% WACC = (0.4 × 20) + (0.1 × 15) + (0.5 × 10) = 8 + 1.5 + 5 = 14.5%

CAPM Required Return = Rrf + β × (Rm – Rrf) [Rrf = risk-free; β = beta; Rm = market return]

DIVIDEND GROWTH MODEL RRR = (D₁ / P₀) + g [D₁ = next dividend; P₀ = current price; g = growth rate]
🧠 Capital Structure Theories — The Story

The central question: Does taking on more debt increase or decrease a firm's value?

  • Net Income Approach (Durand): More debt → WACC decreases → Firm value increases. (Debt is cheaper; more debt means lower blended cost.)
  • Net Operating Income Approach (Durand): Capital structure doesn't matter. WACC stays constant regardless of debt. (Equity holders demand more return as risk rises, offsetting cheap debt benefit.)
  • Traditional Approach: There IS an optimal mix. Beyond that point, financial risk causes WACC to rise. U-shaped WACC curve.
  • MM Theorem: Without taxes, capital structure is irrelevant (supports NOI). WITH taxes, debt is better (interest tax shield).
🧠 Remember: Durand = Both NI and NOI
Both the Net Income approach AND Net Operating Income approach were propounded by David Durand. MM (Modigliani-Miller) came later. If the question says "who proposed NI approach", answer = Durand. If MM — that's the irrelevance theorem.
C·4 Bond Valuation & YTM
BOND PRICE Price = I × PVIFA(kd, n) + F × PVIF(kd, n) [I = annual coupon; F = face value; kd = required return; n = years]

APPROXIMATE YTM YTM ≈ {I + (M – P)/n} ÷ (0.6P + 0.4M) [I = annual interest; M = maturity/face value; P = market price; n = years]

CURRENT YIELD Current Yield = Annual Coupon ÷ Current Market Price
🚨 Bond Price vs YTM Relationship

This is INVERSE: When interest rates rise → Bond prices FALL. When rates fall → prices RISE. This is because a bond's fixed coupon becomes more/less attractive relative to new market rates.

Also: Duration — longer maturity = higher duration = more interest rate sensitivity. Zero coupon bond: Duration = Maturity (highest sensitivity).

🚨 Currency Swap vs Interest Rate Swap

In an Interest Rate Swap (IRS), parties exchange only interest cash flows (fixed vs floating) — NO principal exchange. The notional principal is just for calculating interest.

In a Currency Swap, principal IS exchanged — both at the beginning AND at termination. This is the key distinguishing fact the exam tests. Don't confuse the two.

🧠 Options — Right Without Obligation

Call Option: Right to BUY the asset at the strike price. Buyer profits if price RISES above strike.
Put Option: Right to SELL the asset at the strike price. Buyer profits if price FALLS below strike.

The option BUYER pays a premium. The option SELLER (writer) has the obligation and collects the premium. The buyer's maximum loss = premium paid. The seller's maximum gain = premium received.

MODULE D Costing, BEP & Taxation HIGH YIELD
🧠 Marginal Costing — The Decision-Making Tool

The core idea: Fixed costs exist regardless of production. So when deciding whether to produce one MORE unit, only variable costs matter. The extra revenue from that unit minus its variable cost = Contribution. This contribution first covers fixed costs, then becomes profit.

Think of a cinema hall. Fixed cost (rent, electricity) = ₹50,000. Each ticket sold contributes ₹200 (price) - ₹50 (variable cost) = ₹150 contribution. Need 50,000/150 = 334 tickets to break even. Every ticket after that = ₹150 pure profit.

THE CORE FORMULA CHAIN Contribution = Sales – Variable Cost P/V Ratio = (Contribution ÷ Sales) × 100 = (Selling Price – Variable Cost) ÷ Selling Price × 100

BEP (units) = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution per unit BEP (₹) = Fixed Cost ÷ P/V Ratio

Margin of Safety = (Actual Sales – BEP Sales) ÷ Actual Sales × 100

Profit = Margin of Safety × P/V Ratio Or: Profit = (Actual Sales – BEP Sales) × Contribution per unit
📝 BEP Worked Example

Selling price = ₹100, Variable cost = ₹60, Fixed cost = ₹2,00,000.

  • Contribution per unit = 100 – 60 = ₹40
  • P/V Ratio = 40/100 × 100 = 40%
  • BEP (units) = 2,00,000 ÷ 40 = 5,000 units
  • BEP (₹) = 5,000 × 100 = ₹5,00,000 OR 2,00,000 ÷ 0.40 = ₹5,00,000 ✓
  • If actual sales = 7,000 units: Margin of Safety = (7000–5000)/7000 × 100 = 28.6%
  • Profit = 2,000 units × ₹40 = ₹80,000
🚨 P/V Ratio Trap

Students confuse P/V Ratio with Profit/Volume or Profit/Sales. It's actually Profit-Volume Ratio but it's calculated as Contribution/Sales, NOT Profit/Sales. Contribution includes both fixed cost coverage AND profit. This distinction matters enormously.

Also: Higher P/V ratio = better (more contribution per rupee of sales). Higher margin of safety = more cushion against losses.

D·2 Standard Costing & Variances
🧠 The Variance Intuition

Standard costing sets a BENCHMARK. Variances show WHERE and WHY actual differed from standard. Every variance is either Favourable (F) — reality was better than plan — or Adverse (A) — reality was worse.

The price variance asks: "Did we pay the right price?" The usage variance asks: "Did we use the right amount?"

MATERIAL VARIANCES Material Price Variance (MPV) = (Std Price – Actual Price) × Actual Qty Material Usage Variance (MUV) = (Std Qty – Actual Qty) × Std Price Total Material Variance = MPV + MUV = (Std Cost – Actual Cost)

LABOUR VARIANCES Labour Rate Variance (LRV) = (Std Rate – Actual Rate) × Actual Hours Labour Efficiency Variance (LEV) = (Std Hours – Actual Hours) × Std Rate

SIGN RULE If Std > Actual → FAVOURABLE | If Actual > Std → ADVERSE
🧠 Variance Memory Trick
All variances follow: (Standard – Actual) × [quantity denominator]
Price Variance → "per unit" = Actual Qty
Usage/Efficiency → "per item" = Std Price / Std Rate
If Standard is BIGGER → Favourable (we did better than planned).
D·3 GST — Key Facts for Exam HIGH YIELD
🧠 GST Structure — The Big Picture

GST replaced 17+ central and state indirect taxes (Excise, VAT, Service Tax, etc.) with ONE unified tax from 1 July 2017. It's a destination-based tax — tax goes to the state where goods are CONSUMED, not where they are produced.

The dual GST model: Centre collects CGST, State collects SGST on intra-state supplies. IGST covers inter-state (collected by Centre, then shared with destination state).

🧠 TDS vs TCS — The Distinction

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source): The person making the PAYMENT deducts tax before paying. E.g., employer deducts TDS from salary before paying employee. The payer is the deductor, needs TAN.

TCS (Tax Collected at Source): The person making the SALE collects tax from the buyer at the time of sale. E.g., seller of scrap/minerals collects TCS from buyer. The seller is the collector.

Both are advance collection of income tax — the final tax liability is adjusted against TDS/TCS when the taxpayer files return.
